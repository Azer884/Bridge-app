# Wet Edge

Wet Edge is the movement of the moment between "not yet" and "done". It lives on the line where a roller has just passed: one side raw and ledgered, the other side whole, saturated, finished. Everything in the work is organised around that single boundary, and the boundary is never perfectly straight. It is scalloped by the nap of the roller, and that small imperfection is the proof of a human hand.

Space is a wall seen square-on. The field is cool plaster, quiet and flat, divided by a rhythm of horizontal courses that read like ledger lines or masonry. On one side the courses are plural, uneven and legible, each its own length, each its own measure. On the other they dissolve into one continuous slab of ultramarine. The composition is a transaction from many to one, and the eye should feel the weight of that resolution before reading a single word.

Colour is held to a painter's short list: plaster, ink, ultramarine, masking-tape yellow, and a trace of sage. Ultramarine is spent once, in the finished slab, and nowhere else at that intensity. Yellow appears only as small units of measure, the way tape marks a boundary on a skirting board. Nothing is gradient for the sake of mood; tone shifts only where a real coat would shift.

Typography is clinical and sparse, set like a specification sheet: a handful of small labels in a lean grotesque, aligned to the courses they describe, plus one large figure inside the finished field. Text never decorates. It names the measure on the left and states the single result on the right, and then it stops.

Craftsmanship is the whole point. Every course is spaced to the pixel, every scallop on the wet edge is drawn rather than generated twice, every margin is held to the same measure. The piece should read as the product of countless hours of patient refinement by a master of the discipline: calm from a distance, rewarding up close, and impossible to improve by adding anything.
